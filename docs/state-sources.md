## Notes

- MO: RSMo 167.031 no longer holds the home-school hours; SB 727 (2024) moved them to RSMo 167.012 (effective Aug 28, 2025).
- NY: 900/990 hours and 180-day equivalency confirmed via NYC DOE (official district) documents quoting 8 NYCRR 100.10(f); nysed.gov regulation page itself returned redirect errors.
- NH: RSA 193-A:6 statute page shows repeal effective July 1, 2026; the chapter index still lists it. Confirm before shipping.
- KY: home schools follow the public-school term (KRS 158.080); 185 days / 1,062 hours come from KRS 158.070 definitions.
- WV: 180 days comes from W. Va. Code 18-5-45, referenced by 18-8-1(c).
- WA: 1,000 hours is a school-wide average for grades 1-12 (450 for K) via RCW 28A.195.010, referenced by 28A.225.010(4).
- NE: JSON requiredHours uses the secondary figure (1,080); elementary is 1,032. RI hours figure is RIDE's statement of the public-school equivalent, not a homeschool-specific statute number.
- Unverified (official pages blocked or statute text not retrievable): AL, AK, KS, MS, NH.
- Retrieval date for all rows: 2026-09-09.


## 2026-09-13 verification pass — notes that matter

**New Hampshire — the law changed.** RSA 193-A:6, which required a portfolio, reading
log and annual evaluation, was repealed by 2026 ch. 297 s. 10(I), effective July 1, 2026,
and nothing replaced it. The same act ordered the expedited repeal of administrative
rules Ed 315. New Hampshire now requires essentially nothing; notification is optional
except when withdrawing from a public school. **The NH Department of Education website
still describes the repealed rules**, so the app is currently more accurate than the
state's own page. Verified against the statute and the enacted session law
(HB 1268), not the department site.

**Kansas — the number is a benchmark by reference, not a direct mandate.** K.S.A.
72-3120 requires a term "substantially equivalent" to the local district's, and sets no
figure itself. The 186 days / 1,116 hours comes from K.S.A. 72-3115, which by its own
terms binds school districts. Days and hours are alternatives, and kindergarten (465
hours) and grade 12 (181 days / 1,086 hours) differ. Encoded as a default target with
the "substantially equivalent" language in the note, not as a pass/fail threshold.
Do not conflate with K.S.A. 72-3120(g), a separate religious-alternative program that
*does* impose monthly record filing — that is the likely source of incorrect "Kansas
requires records" claims in secondary sources.

**Alabama — the numbers belong to the private-tutor option only.** The 140 days /
3 hours per day and the daily register of work are Ala. Code 16-28-5, which applies to
the certificated private tutor. The church-school option that most Alabama homeschoolers
actually use carries none of them. Days/hours left null so the majority case is not
misled; the tutor figures live in the note. attendanceLog left null because the
church-school enrollment mechanics in 16-28-7 could not be read verbatim (the
legislature's ALISON site is a JavaScript-only app that serves no statute text to a
fetcher). Verified from the official ALSDE attendance manual and homeschool FAQ.

**Alaska — genuinely nothing.** AS 14.30.010(b)(12) is a single unconditional clause;
every other exemption in (b) carries qualifying language and this one carries none. The
180-day term in AS 14.03.030 binds districts. Note that district correspondence programs
are a *separate* track under (b)(10)(B) and do add an individual learning plan,
certificated-teacher grading and state testing — a possible future feature, not a
record-keeping requirement for parent-directed families.

**Mississippi — read from MDE's published copy of the statute.** legislature.ms.gov was
unreachable (robots/TLS failures); the text came from the Mississippi Department of
Education's own PDF of Miss. Code Ann. 37-13-91 plus the official certificate-of-enrollment
form, both on mdek12.org. The PDF is an excerpt covering the home-school provisions, so
the false values for attendance log and portfolio rest on that excerpt and the form being
silent, not on a full read of every subsection.
